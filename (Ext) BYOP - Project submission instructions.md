# **Welcome\! Let's get your project in**

**You build what you'd build anyway, with an AI coding assistant, and get paid for your time.** This form is the first step into BYOP: tell us about the project you want to bring, and once it's cleared, you're on your way to your first paid session.

## **Why you'll like it**

- **Your project, your code.** You keep full ownership of your project, including the code the assistant writes for you in it.  
- **Free tokens while you work.** Your AI usage during the project is covered: no subscription, no API bill.  
- **Paid time on work you'd do anyway.** Ship the feature, fix the bug, finally write those docs.  
- **Your judgment counts.** At each step you see two versions of the assistant's response. You pick the one that worked with you better and say why in a few sentences.  
- **Remote.** Work from your own computer, on your own schedule.

## **How you get in**

1. **Submit your projects** with the form in this Labelbox project: one task per project, about 10 minutes each.  
2. **We review each one** and select the projects that fit best.  
3. **For each selected project, you sign the contributor terms** and certify it.  
4. **You set up the assistant** with the access we send you.  
5. **You start building.**

Spots in the first group are limited, and **we select projects**, not just people. A complete, accurate form for each project is the fastest way in.

## **What makes a great project**

We're looking for real projects with real work ahead:

- **A software project you personally own,** such as a side project, a tool you've been meaning to finish, or a product you're building on your own. Open-source maintainers are especially welcome.  
- **Active and real:** your own commits over time, ideally with signs of use (releases, users, issues, a deployment).  
- **Room to grow:** at least 20 hours of work you plan to do anyway: features, bugs, refactors, tests, docs.

**Submit all your eligible projects.** 

We review and select each project on its own, so every eligible project you send is another chance to be selected. Have a side project, a tool and an open-source library you maintain? Submit all three, one task each. Sending more doesn't commit you to working on all of them.

## **Not eligible**

To keep everyone's work safe to share, these projects can't be used:

- Work for an employer, client or university, or built on their time, equipment, money or confidential information.  
- Anything under an NDA or another confidentiality agreement.  
- Code owned by a company or startup, even your own (usually not eligible: if yours is, say so in the form).  
- Work for, or that benefits, a company that builds frontier AI models.  
- Building, training, fine-tuning, evaluating or benchmarking AI models, or creating data to do that.  
- AI inference, model serving, compute or machine-learning infrastructure.  
- Security exploit tooling: finding or exploiting vulnerabilities, malware or other offensive cyber tools.  
- Projects that process other people's personal data, or regulated data (for example health or financial records).  
- Anything illegal or harmful, or code you don't have the right to share.

Not sure about yours? Ask in the General Questions thread before you submit.

## **Get your repository ready**

Your project must be in a **public GitHub repository**. Our reviewers read it to confirm what you tell us, so let it show your work at its best:

- [ ] **A README** that says what the project does, who it's for, what it's built with and how to run it.  
- [ ] **A LICENSE file** in the top folder, with the license you pick in the form (see the table below).  
- [ ] **Your real history.** Keep your commits as they are: don't squash them, re-upload the code to a new repository, or submit a fork or a template copy. Make your commits with an email linked to your GitHub account, so we can see they're yours.  
- [ ] **No secrets.** No passwords, API keys, tokens or private keys anywhere in the repository or its history. Keep `.env` and other secrets files out (list them in `.gitignore`) and use placeholders such as `.env.example`. If a key was ever committed, revoke it and remove it from the history before you submit. We scan every repository.  
- [ ] **No real personal or customer data.** Use made-up sample data instead.  
- [ ] **Your plans in view (recommended).** Open issues, a `ROADMAP.md` or a TODO list in the README. It's the easiest way to show us the work ahead.

> **Private project?** For now this form only accepts public GitHub repositories. If you'd rather keep your project private, ask in the General Questions thread before you submit. If you make it public, add an accepted license first: a public repository with no license can't be accepted.

## **Filling in the form**

**Project name:** as it appears in the repository.

**Repository link:** the repository's main page, like `https://github.com/your-name/your-project`. Not a branch, file or pull request. For an open-source project, the main repository, not your fork.

**Short description:** two to four sentences in your own words: what it does, who uses it, and what it's built with. For example: "A command-line tool that syncs my reading list between e-readers. I use it daily and a few hundred people have installed it. Written in Rust, with a small SQLite store." Specific beats generic: it helps us match you quickly.

**License:** the one in your LICENSE file. It must match what the repository shows.

**Your relationship to the project:**

- **I created it and I am the only owner:** you started it and nobody else owns any part of it.  
- **I co-own it with other people:** someone else owns part of it, such as a co-founder or a friend who wrote part of it. Tell us who in the comments; we're confirming how these are handled.  
- **Open-source project I maintain or contribute to:** a public project with other contributors, where you're a maintainer or a significant contributor.  
- **I built it for an employer, client or school:** these can't be used. Please submit a different project.  
- **Other:** describe your situation and we'll check it.

**Owned by a company, partnership or other entity (including your own startup)?** Answer **Yes** if the code belongs to a company, even one you own alone (for example, it lives under your startup's GitHub organization). If Yes, name the company and your role (for example, "Founder and sole owner of Acme Labs LLC"). If you're **Not sure**, tell us why in the comments.

**Open-source projects only:**

- **Your repository username:** the GitHub username your commits are made under.  
- **Your role:**  
  - **Maintainer:** you're listed as one (for example in `MAINTAINERS` or `CODEOWNERS`), merge other people's pull requests, publish releases, or have write access.  
  - **Significant contributor:** a steady record of substantial pull requests, merged by other maintainers, over several months.  
  - **Occasional contributor:** a few small contributions. This won't qualify, so consider bringing a different project.  
- **Your role and contributions:** how long you've been involved, what you've worked on, and links to a few merged pull requests. We check this against the repository's history.

**Anything else?** Optional: an answer you weren't sure about, where the project is deployed or used, or where your plans are listed.

## **After you submit**

We review each project on its own, so you'll hear back about each one with one of these:

- **Selected:** next come the contributor terms for that project, then your setup.  
- **Needs extra approval:** for GPL-family or unlisted licenses, or anything we can't confirm from the repository. We'll tell you when it's approved; don't start on it until then.  
- **Fix and resubmit:** something small to fix, such as a missing LICENSE file or a secret in the history. We'll tell you what to change.  
- **Not eligible:** this project can't be used, but you're welcome to submit a different one.

Please keep your answers true and up to date: they have to match the project certification you'll sign. If something changes later (license, ownership, a new employer or client claim), tell us right away.

Please never post API keys or other private details in the form, on Discord or in your repository.
